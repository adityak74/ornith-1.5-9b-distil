Quantizing Ornith-1.5-9B, a 9B hybrid-attention reasoning model, to 4.7 bits costs 4.6 points of MMLU and 3.7 of HumanEval. We set out to recover that loss by distillation on one 64 GB Apple M4 Pro and tried twelve things. The first worked: two-teacher sequence-level distillation with rejection sampling, trained in bf16 and quantized last, scores +5.5 MMLU and +3.0 HumanEval over the quantized baseline at identical size and bit width, beats the 8-bit build and matches its 35B teacher on MMLU. Nine training-side variants did not, and a randomised control showed a second pass from the best checkpoint costs 1-4 points whatever it trains on. Diagnosing why the first run worked was the real result: most of what quantization costs this model is not reasoning but the ability to stop. The 4-bit build fails to reach an answer on 24% of HumanEval problems its parent finishes; 22 of the 28 problems distillation recovered were budget exhaustions. Two decode-time repairs follow and need no training. HALT, a Harness-Applied Limit on Thinking, in its hard form closes the reasoning block at the budget (+10.4 HumanEval on the stock build). In its soft form a bias on the </think> logit grows with position, so stopping gets steadily cheaper: +18.9 points on the stock build (108 to 139 of 164) and +10.4 on the distilled one at 2,048 tokens, with no correct item becoming wrong; at 2,048 tokens it outscores the full-precision parent at 4,096. The ramp is not a quantization repair: the bf16 parent, the 35B teacher, a dense Qwen3.8-27B and an MoE Qwen3.6-35B gain by the same fraction of what truncated, the last from 27 to 147 of 164; quantization only raises how often it happens. Termination is a decoding-time property before it is a weights property, and a benchmark that does not report truncation is partly reporting its token budget. We release the pipeline, checkpoints, per-item records and a 54-section decision log, and a chunkwise training path for gated DeltaNet in MLX.
